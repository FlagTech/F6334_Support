# Apple-inspired minimal presentation style

Apply this as an original minimalist visual direction. Do not copy Apple trademarks, logos, product photography, marketing copy, or proprietary slide templates.

## Canvas and hierarchy

- Use 16:9 widescreen.
- Use equal left and right margins and generous, deliberate whitespace.
- Build one flat composition per slide instead of a grid of UI cards.
- Keep the title slide to the title, reporting period, and optional team name or small company logo.
- Give every slide one dominant statement, metric, chart, or table.
- Vary silhouettes modestly while keeping alignment, spacing, and hierarchy consistent.

## Palette

- Background: `#F5F5F7`
- Primary text: `#1D1D1F`
- Secondary text: `#6E6E73`
- Divider: `#D2D2D7`
- Default accent: `#0071E3`

Use the company palette when supplied. Limit the default deck to the neutral palette plus one accent color. Use red, amber, and green only when they encode actual status and add a text label so color is never the sole signal.

## Typography

- Use Aptos Display for deck and slide titles.
- Use Aptos for body text.
- Fall back to Arial when Aptos is unavailable.
- Use at least 50 pt for the deck title, 35 pt for slide titles, 24 pt for subheads, and 16 pt for body text.
- Use 40–60 pt for a dominant metric when appropriate.
- Prefer short sentences and compact labels. Never shrink text below the minimums to fit.
- Keep titles on one line; shorten the title or change the layout if it wraps.

## Visual content

- Use no photograph by default.
- Use at most one meaningful photograph across a four-to-six-slide deck.
- Do not add decorative stock photos, image collages, repeated images, ornamental icons, device mockups, or glossy gradients.
- Prefer native charts, compact tables, timelines, progress markers, thin rules, and large numbers.
- Use a chart only when the data supports a comparison or trend. Label it directly and keep axes and legends minimal.
- Avoid dense dashboards, card walls, pills, tabs, buttons, and interactive-product styling.

## Spacing and detail

- Use a consistent spacing rhythm and align text to a small number of vertical guides.
- Prefer thin separators and subtle contrast over boxes and heavy borders.
- Limit each slide to one primary accent treatment.
- Keep footers and page numbers quiet and consistent.
- Remove any element that does not improve comprehension.

## Quality checks

- Inspect each slide at full size, not only in a montage.
- Confirm no title wraps unexpectedly and no body text feels crowded.
- Confirm every chart and table matches the approved source data.
- Confirm status colors, labels, and meanings are consistent.
- Confirm the deck contains no unresolved `待補`, internal production note, or approval instruction.
